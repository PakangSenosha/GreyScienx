## Post 01 — The forty-year echo

A labour-market failure at 20 can reappear in the pension budget at 60.

South Africa’s youth unemployment rate was 47.4% in Q2 2026: 5.0 million people aged 15–34 were unemployed and 5.6 million were employed. That is the observed warning. This paper asks a different question: what happens if weak access to work hardens into a lifetime contribution history?

The GreyScienx scenario model follows six representative lives from age 20 to 100. They share the same age-specific earning opportunity. What changes is when work begins, whether it stays formal and covered, how wages recover, and how much reaches retirement saving and tax revenue.

Three results stand out.

First, temporary unemployment is costly but recoverable. Five jobless years from 20 to 24 leave R3.99m at 60, 18% below the stable formal worker’s R4.84m.

Second, timing and coverage matter as much as years worked. Two profiles each work 30 years, yet the worker who contributed in their twenties reaches R3.33m versus R3.09m for entry only at 30. A mostly informal profile works 32 years but reaches just R0.91m.

Third, retirement reveals a public gap accumulated over decades. For illustrative cohorts of one million people, the excluded path leaves a R1.80tn weaker lifetime fiscal balance. About R1.25tn is missing modeled tax revenue and R0.56tn is additional old-age grant spending.

These are conditional scenarios in constant 2026 rand, not forecasts. The grant is an analytical top-up, healthcare costs are assumed equal across profiles, cohort mixes are illustrative, and lifetime fiscal totals are undiscounted.

The policy sequence matters: restore stable, productive and covered work before asking a zero wage to carry a higher pension contribution. Protecting the first contribution years is cheaper than reconstructing them late.

## Post 07 — The catch-up cliff

Catch-up becomes brutal. At a 3% real return, entry at 30 requires saving 24% of salary to match a 15% saver from 20. Entry at 40 needs 51%; entry at 45, 86%.

## Post 08 — Retirement reveals the gap

Retirement does not create the fiscal gap; it reveals it. For one million modeled lives, age-59 net flows are +R114bn/+R74bn. At 60 they flip to –R14bn/–R28bn.
