# Verified source ledger

Access checked 20 September 2026. Links in the commissioning brief were treated as leads and checked against the underlying publication. “Use” records the exact role in the paper; “limit” records why the source cannot bear a stronger claim.

## Official statistics and administrative evidence

1. **Statistics South Africa. _Census 2022 Statistical Release_, P0301.4 (2023).**  
   URL: <https://census.statssa.gov.za/assets/documents/2022/P03014_Census_2022_Statistical_Release.pdf>  
   Use: population shares (Black African 81.4%, Coloured 8.2%, White 7.3%, Indian/Asian 2.7%); 17.8 million households; 41.6% owned and fully paid, 6.6% owned but not paid, 23.2% rented and 25.6% occupied rent-free; formal/traditional/informal dwelling and provincial service comparisons.  
   Limit: tenure is reported for the occupied dwelling and is not proof of title, market value, inheritance, individual control or collateral access.

2. **Statistics South Africa. _Living Conditions of Households in South Africa 2014/15_, P0310 (2017).**  
   URL: <https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0310/P03102014.pdf>  
   Use: 7.135% rental-yield imputation for owner-occupied housing expenditure.  
   Limit: survey imputation, not a realised market rent or net yield; mortgage and imputed-rent data quality warnings apply.

3. **Statistics South Africa. _Census of Commercial Agriculture 2017_ (2020).**  
   URL: <https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/Report-11-02-01/CoCA%202017%20Fact%20Sheets.pdf>  
   Use: 40,122 commercial farming units, 46.4 million hectares, R332.8 billion gross income and 77% farmer-owned land.  
   Limit: commercial farming census, not all household or communal agriculture; gross industry income is not inherited farm profit.

4. **Department of Rural Development and Land Reform. _Land Audit Report: Phase II - Private Land Ownership by Race, Gender and Nationality_ (2017).**  
   URL: <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201802/landauditreport13feb2018.pdf>  
   Use: deeds coverage; ownership by entity type; the racial distribution of farms and agricultural holdings held by identifiable private individuals.  
   Limit: race could not be assigned to company, trust, institutional, communal, state or many co-owned holdings. The often-quoted 72/15/5/4 percentages describe only 37.0 million hectares held by private individuals, not all South African land.

5. **Department of Agriculture, Land Reform and Rural Development / South African Government. “Get Deeds Registry information.”**  
   URL: <https://www.gov.za/services/services-residents/place-live/get-deeds-registry-information>  
   Use: registry scope, including registered owner, purchase price, conditions, servitudes and mortgage bonds.  
   Limit: describes registered rights and records, not off-register occupation or living customary rights.

## Wealth, inheritance and legal administration

6. **Simson, Rebecca, and Mina Mahmoudzadeh. _Inherited Wealth in Post-Apartheid South Africa: New Perspectives from Probate Records_. LSE International Inequalities Institute Working Paper 146 (2024).**  
   URL: <https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/125939/3/III-WP-146.pdf>  
   Use: pooled probate threshold estimates; estate-to-death ratios; NIDS comparison; 2019 estate geography; explicit bias analysis.  
   Limit: names are used to infer population group; reporting differs across groups; deaths select older people; estate values are observed only in threshold bands; addresses do not prove the asset inherited.

7. **Chelwa, Grieve, Mashekwa Maboshe, and Darrick Hamilton. “The Racial Wealth Gap in South Africa and the United States.” _Review of Political Economy_ 36(2), 423-440 (2024).**  
   DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1080/09538259.2024.2318962>  
   Use: NIDS Wave 5 median household net worth of R70,100 for Black households and R1,364,900 for White households in 2017; persistence of the descriptive gap across education and income.  
   Limit: household median, not individual wealth; descriptive rather than a causal estimate; focus is Black-White comparison.

8. **Chatterjee, Aroop, Leo Czajka, and Amory Gethin. “Wealth Inequality in South Africa, 1993-2017.” _World Bank Economic Review_ 36(1), 19-36 (2022).**  
   DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1093/wber/lhab012>  
   Use: top 10% own over 85% and top 1% about 55% of personal wealth; housing composition; NIDS coverage limitations; within-group inequality.  
   Limit: distributional-account reconstruction combines surveys, tax and macro balance sheets; it does not identify inherited property.

9. **Bolt, Maxim. “Homeownership, Legal Administration, and the Uncertainties of Inheritance in South Africa's Townships: Apartheid's Legal Shadows.” _African Affairs_ 120(479), 219-241 (2021).**  
   DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1093/afraf/adab001>  
   Use: qualitative evidence on unreported estates, family-house claims, administrative burden and formal/informal succession.  
   Limit: ethnographic and institutional evidence, not a national incidence estimate.

10. **Clark, Michael, and Nolundi Luwaya. _Communal Land Tenure 1994-2017_. High Level Panel commissioned report, Parliament of South Africa (2017).**  
    URL: <https://www.parliament.gov.za/storage/app/media/Pages/2017/october/High_Level_Panel/Commissioned_Report_land/Commisioned_Report_on_Tenure_Reform_LARC.pdf>  
    Use: legal and social dimensions of tenure security; estimated scale of former-homeland residence; limits of private-title analogies.  
    Limit: commissioned legal-policy report; the “almost 17 million” estimate predates Census 2022.

11. **Casale, Daniela, and Adeola Oyenubi. “Intra-Couple Wealth Inequality in South Africa.” _South African Journal of Economics_ 94(1), e70014 (2026).**  
    DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1111/saje.70014>  
    Use: warning that household wealth is not necessarily equally controlled; evidence of within-household gender gaps.  
    Limit: individual allocation and couple sample; not an inheritance-incidence study.

## Housing geography, market depth and collateral

12. **Centre for Affordable Housing Finance in Africa. _South Africa Housing Market Report 2024_ (2024).**  
    Archived copy: <https://www.wcpdf.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/CAHF_V8-National-Property-Market-Report-2024-Final.pdf>  
    Use: 2023 registered housing stock, value segments, transaction counts, government-subsidised housing resales and bonded shares.  
    Limit: Lightstone-cleaned deeds data cover the formal registered market; backyard units, informal dwellings and title-backlog properties are excluded; valuations are modelled and segments are nominal.

13. **Kirsten, Charlene, and Leila Patel. “Spatial inertia and the racial stratification of housing wealth in post-Apartheid South Africa.” SALDRU (2025), based on NIDS Waves 1-5.**  
    URL: <https://saldru.uct.ac.za/articles/2025-11-26-spatial-inertia-and-racial-stratification-housing-wealth-post-apartheid-south-africa>  
    Use: longitudinal descriptive evidence on settlement types and housing-value pathways; 27-32% of the NIDS sample in Tribal Authority Areas; 2008 and 2017 imputed-rent contrasts within urban formal areas.  
    Limit: research article summary rather than a full peer-reviewed causal paper; imputed rent and self-reported housing values; racialised market access is associated, not experimentally assigned.

14. **Naude, Wim. “Is there a spatial mismatch in South Africa's metropolitan labour market?” _Cities_ 25(5), 268-276 (2008).**  
    DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cities.2008.04.004>  
    Use: evidence that distance from the centre was associated with Black unemployment after income and education controls in some metros.  
    Limit: older cross-sectional evidence; controls do not establish causality and metropolitan patterns differ.

15. **OECD. _Economic Surveys: South Africa 2022_, productivity chapter (2022).**  
    URL: <https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-south-africa-2022_d6a7301d-en/full-report/component-6.html>  
    Use: immovable property can support collateral for young firms, conditional on defined rights and timely recovery.  
    Limit: policy assessment, not an estimated causal return to collateral.

16. **South African Reserve Bank. _Interlinkages between South African households' financial position and the domestic financial system_ (2023).**  
    URL: <https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/what-we-do/financial-stability/Interlinkages%20between%20SA%20households%27%20financial%20position%20and%20the%20domestic%20financial%20system.pdf>  
    Use: real estate's balance-sheet role and occasional use as collateral; downside risk when prices fall.  
    Limit: financial-stability analysis, not household-level causal evidence.

17. **OECD. _Economic Surveys: South Africa 2025_, workforce integration chapter (2025).**  
    URL: <https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-south-africa-2025_7e6a132a-en/full-report/enhancing-job-creation-and-workforce-integration-in-a-changing-economy_e1449aca.html>  
    Use: 2020 commute-time comparisons by mode and the spatial-access mechanism.  
    Limit: mode averages do not identify the wealth return to any property.

## Agricultural value and productive conditions

18. **Zantsi, Siphe, et al. “Trends in land prices paid for farms redistributed under the proactive land acquisition strategy in South Africa (2005-2018).” _Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems_ (2025).**  
    DOI: <https://doi.org/10.3389/fsufs.2025.1682797>  
    Use: farm price associations with size, location, water rights and extension; finance valuation methods; limited water rights among PLAS beneficiaries.  
    Limit: PLAS acquisitions are selected and not nationally representative; price associations are not causal effects of title or water.

19. **Middelberg, Sanlie. “Agricultural land valuation methods used by financiers: The case of South Africa.” _Agrekon_ 53(3), 101-115 (2014).**  
    DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1080/03031853.2014.922033>  
    Use: financiers' use of comparable sales, income capitalisation and collateral valuation; lack of uniform public farm-sale statistics.  
    Limit: valuation-practice study, not a realised return series.

20. **Chikozho, Claudious, Rirhanzu Managa, and Takalani Dabata. “Ensuring access to water for food production by emerging farmers in South Africa.” _Water SA_ 46(2) (2020).**  
    DOI: <https://doi.org/10.17159/wsa/2020.v46.i2.8237>  
    Use: qualitative evidence that reliable irrigation water is central to productive use of redistributed farms.  
    Limit: case studies in Bela-Bela and Greater Sekhukhune; not a national income effect.

## Classification used in the manuscript

- **Causal evidence:** none of the core South African sources identifies the causal effect of inheriting a property type on lifetime wealth.
- **Descriptive evidence:** Census, NIDS, probate, deeds, land-audit, commute and agricultural studies.
- **Modeled results:** every 20/40/60-year trajectory, channel decomposition, threshold probability, population-group intake distribution and agricultural counterfactual produced by `model.py`.
