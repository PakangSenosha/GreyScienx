# Inherited Geography: The Racial Property-Wealth Dividend

## Central question

How much of South Africa's persistent racial wealth gap can be explained by differences in the type, location, tenure, marketability, and appreciation of property inherited across generations?

The proposed mechanism is measurable: apartheid allocated racial groups not merely different quantities of property, but different kinds of property in different locations. Those assets have subsequently had different abilities to appreciate, generate rent, secure credit, finance education or enterprise, and transfer wealth.

Do not describe rural property as worthless. Distinguish market value, liquidity, collateral value, income value, and residential or social use value.

## Study scope

The study should examine:

- Probability of receiving any inheritance, residential property, land, or productive property by population group
- Value and age of inheritance
- Historically white suburban property, historically Black township property, former homeland or rural property, and commercial agricultural land
- Communal tenure, customary tenure, leasehold, and freehold title
- Market value versus use value
- Saleability, mortgageability, collateral value, rental potential, rental yield, and appreciation
- Location relative to employment, infrastructure, schools, transport, and municipal services
- Municipal property-market depth, transaction volumes, and liquidity
- Houses versus vacant land, residential land versus productive farms, and farm income
- Multiple heirs, subdivision, informal inheritance, title problems, intestate succession, disputes, and estate delays
- Maintenance costs, rates, taxes, and inherited mortgage debt
- Inter vivos transfers, deposit assistance, and first-home support
- Business formation, education, mobility, and financial risk-taking enabled by family property
- Intergenerational compounding over 20, 40, and 60 years
- Whether initial property differences widen when descendants subsequently have similar education, earnings, household size, and saving behaviour

## Property-value framework

Represent inherited property as a bundle of economically distinct values rather than a single price:

- Market or resale value
- Residential use value and avoided housing expenditure
- Collateral value
- Rental-income value
- Liquidity
- Expected appreciation
- Productive income where relevant
- Social, family, and customary value that is not captured by ordinary market prices

The model should recognise that a titled township house, a communal rural family home, a suburban Johannesburg property, and a commercial farm can all be called property while transferring very different quantities of liquid and leverageable capital.

## Inheritance multiplier

Model the channels through which inherited property can affect later wealth:

- Capital appreciation
- Rent avoided
- Rental income
- Collateral for enterprise or another property
- Lower borrowing costs
- Greater capacity to absorb risk or unemployment
- Education and geographic mobility
- Earlier acquisition of subsequent property
- Transfers to the next generation

Separate direct compounding of inherited capital from these behavioural and institutional channels.

## Agricultural-property module

Compare technically similar land areas under different legal and productive conditions. Include title, agricultural income, collateral, water access, infrastructure, resale markets, operating capital, and appreciation. Clearly disclose the limitations of racial attribution in land-audit data, especially company, trust, institutional, communal, and state holdings.

## Matched-descendant counterfactual

Match representative descendants on age, education, occupation, salary, household size, and saving behaviour. Vary only inherited property conditions, using empirically estimated distributions where possible.

Illustrative trajectories may include:

1. No inherited property
2. Inherited rural or communal residence
3. Inherited titled township home
4. Inherited middle-value suburban property
5. Inherited high-value property or a small portfolio
6. Inherited productive agricultural property

Do not treat illustrative nominal values as empirical facts. Estimate parameter ranges from Deeds Registry data, probate research, NIDS, municipal valuation rolls, Stats SA, agricultural-land sources, and peer-reviewed work. Where representative microdata are unavailable, use transparent scenarios and report the resulting limitation.

Use distributional or Monte Carlo modeling rather than assigning one value to every member of a population group. Outputs should include the distributions of wealth at later ages, the probability of exceeding meaningful wealth thresholds, and decompositions of the contribution from appreciation, housing savings, rental income, collateral, and other channels.

## Candidate evidence to verify

Treat these as research leads. Check the original publication, definitions, sample, price year, population denominator, and methodological limitations before using any number:

- Statistics South Africa, Census 2022 statistical release: https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/P03014_Census_2022_Statistical_Release.pdf
- *The Racial Wealth Gap in South Africa and the United States*: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/09538259.2024.2318962
- *Inherited wealth in post-apartheid South Africa: New perspectives from probate records*: https://www.datafirst.uct.ac.za/dataportal/index.php/citations/7854
- SALDRU work on spatial inertia and racial stratification in housing wealth: https://saldru.uct.ac.za/articles/2025-11-26-spatial-inertia-and-racial-stratification-housing-wealth-post-apartheid-south-africa
- South African government land-reform and land-audit material: https://www.gov.za/issues/land-reform

Candidate claims requiring verification include Census 2022 population shares, racial differences in median household wealth, probate-estate coverage and estate locations, imputed-rent gaps within formal urban areas, and racial patterns in individually owned agricultural land.

## Main output

Estimate the distribution of inherited property wealth by population group and property type, then model how initial asset differences may compound into differences in housing wealth, financial wealth, borrowing capacity, enterprise formation, and lifetime net worth across subsequent generations.

Develop and measure **intergenerational property momentum**:

> How much economic momentum can an inherited spatial distribution retain after the laws that originally created it disappear?

The research must allow the estimated mechanism to be smaller, larger, or less identifiable than the starting intuition.

## Editorial and ethical framing

- Keep the manuscript armchair, readable, and thorough; place mathematical machinery in the model code and explain the economics in prose.
- Treat population-group classifications as historically and institutionally produced analytical variables, not innate characteristics.
- Report within-group heterogeneity and overlapping distributions prominently.
- Do not infer individual wealth from race.
- Separate causal evidence, descriptive evidence, and modeled counterfactuals.
- Avoid implying that formal market value exhausts the meaning or welfare value of family land and housing.
- Discuss data omissions, informal succession, communal tenure, survivorship and probate-selection bias, valuation uncertainty, and historical price comparability.

Headline question:

> **Thirty years after apartheid ended, how much of its property geography is still compounding?**
